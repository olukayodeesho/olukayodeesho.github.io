# When AI Writes the Code: How Should We Interview Developers Now?

A candidate submits a flawless take-home assignment.
Clean architecture. Tests included. Sensible naming.

The question most interviewers quietly ask next isn't "Is this good code?"
It's "Did they actually write this?"

That single doubt captures the tension in modern software interviews. AI hasn't broken hiring — it has exposed how outdated many of our assumptions already were.

## The Limits of Take-Home Coding

Take-home assignments became popular because they resemble real work. Developers get time, tools, and space to think — all good things.

But in an AI-enabled workflow, take-home tasks increasingly measure final output, not depth of understanding.

AI can now generate:

- Solid implementations
- Reasonable architecture
- Even basic tests

This doesn't mean the candidate lacks skill. It means the signal is incomplete unless we explore how decisions were made.

## Live Coding Isn't the Silver Bullet Either

Live coding interviews attempt to solve that problem by observing candidates in real time.

They do reveal thinking, communication, and adaptability.
But they also introduce noise:

- Artificial pressure
- Unnatural constraints
- An overemphasis on speed

Many excellent engineers do not perform well while being watched and timed — and most real-world coding doesn't happen that way anyway.

## AI Changed the Definition of "Skill"

The uncomfortable truth is that writing every line from memory is no longer the job.

Modern engineering skill looks more like:

- Asking precise questions
- Evaluating AI-generated code
- Understanding trade-offs and risks
- Debugging and improving imperfect suggestions
- Taking responsibility for the final system

An interview process that pretends AI doesn't exist is testing for a role that no longer exists.

## What Actually Works Better

The strongest signal today comes from combining approaches, not choosing sides.

Effective interviews often include:

- A small, time-boxed take-home task
- A live walkthrough of the solution
- Practical exercises like debugging or refactoring
- Open conversation about how the candidate uses AI

This shifts the focus from who typed the code to who owns the decisions.

## What We're Really Hiring For

Strong engineers aren't defined by syntax speed or memorised patterns.

They consistently show:

- Clear reasoning
- Good judgment
- Ability to explain trade-offs
- Willingness to learn and adapt

AI hasn't reduced the value of these traits. It has made them essential.

## Final Thought

Technical interviews were never meant to identify developers who work without help.

They were meant to find people who can think clearly, build responsibly, and stand behind their work.

If we interview for thinking instead of typing, we'll continue to hire great engineers — even as the tools keep evolving.
